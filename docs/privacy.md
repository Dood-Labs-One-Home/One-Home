# Privacy Direction

## Privacy Is a Core Requirement

One Home is being built around the idea that people should understand what is public, what is shared, and what remains private.

Privacy is especially important for personal memories, journals, identity-linked activity, and future community participation.

The goal is not to hide privacy controls behind complicated settings. The goal is to make privacy understandable and intentional from the beginning.

## Public, Shared, and Private Content

One Home is being organized around three simple privacy levels.

### Public

Public content may include:

* Public profiles
* Community announcements
* Public projects
* Public game activity
* Public achievements or badges
* Shared creator work
* Public event activity

Public content is intended to help people discover projects, games, and communities.

### Shared

Shared content is visible only to people chosen by the owner.

Examples may include:

* A memory shared with selected people
* A private journal entry shared later
* A private link protected with a password
* A creator project shared with a small group
* A community space with limited access

Shared content should remain controlled by the person who created it.

### Private

Private content should remain private by default.

Examples include:

* Personal Night Shots memories
* Private journals
* Uploaded photos
* Personal stories
* Draft content
* Private sharing permissions
* Sensitive identity-linked activity

Private data should not be treated as public social content or used as a default activity feed.

## Night Shots Privacy Direction

Night Shots is the privacy-focused memory and journaling project inside One Home.

Its current prototype supports private local memory features such as:

* Photo memories
* Written stories
* Journals
* Favorites
* Search
* Gallery and journal views
* Sharing controls
* Password-protected sharing links
* Recycle bin and restore behavior

The future direction is to make privacy controls clearer while preserving the emotional and personal nature of memories.

## Identity and Privacy

Dood ID is intended to support one connected identity direction across One Home experiences.

That does not mean every action should become public.

Identity should help people:

* Keep control of their profile
* Connect to experiences they choose
* Carry progress between rooms and games
* Decide how they appear to others
* Control what information is shared

The long-term design should separate identity from unnecessary exposure.

## Midnight Direction

Midnight is being explored as a future privacy layer for One Home and Night Shots.

The intended use is not to move every part of the platform into private systems immediately. Instead, the goal is to identify the areas where privacy-preserving technology provides the most meaningful benefit.

Early privacy-focused milestones may include:

1. Private memory ownership and access controls.
2. Private journal permissions.
3. Selective sharing without exposing unrelated personal data.
4. Privacy-aware identity-linked activity.
5. Clear proof of access or membership without unnecessary disclosure.

## Repository Safety

The public project repository should never include:

* Private Night Shots photos
* Personal journals
* Live user exports
* Wallet seed phrases
* Private keys
* Service-role keys
* Access tokens
* Production environment files
* Personal backups

Only source code, safe public assets, and documentation should be included in the review repository.

## Privacy Principles

* Private should be the default for personal memories.
* People should understand who can see their content.
* Sharing should be reversible when possible.
* Identity should not require unnecessary disclosure.
* Public community activity should remain separate from private personal history.
* Privacy controls should be clear enough for everyday people to use.
